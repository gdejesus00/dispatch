# House style for all three papers

Read this before writing an edition. It applies to the Morning Dispatch, the Cyber Dispatch and the AI Dispatch. The checker enforces lengths and sources; this page is about how the paper reads.

## Headlines

- One idea per headline, in the active voice, present tense: "Poland takes its services platform offline after an attack".
- The lead headline may join two stories with "as" or a semicolon. No other headline does.
- Under 90 characters. No full stop at the end. No questions, no puns, no "Here's what to know".
- Say who did what. Put attribution in the headline when the claim is one side's: "Russia claims Voskresenka; ISW rejects it".
- When an item is older than the window, begin with its date: "From November 9: ...".

## The dek and the bottom line

- The dek adds what the headline left out. It never repeats the headline's words.
- The bottom line is three or four sentences and under 600 characters. A reader who stops there should know the two or three things that matter. It is not a list of everything in the paper.

## Summaries

- Two or three sentences. Lead with what happened, then who says so, then the caveat or the number.
- One fact per sentence where you can. Most sentences under 25 words.
- Name the source inside the sentence when the claim depends on it: "the governor said", "according to Reuters", "one firm's study".
- Numbers: use figures for 10 and above and for all measurements, money and counts in the data; spell out one to nine in prose. Write "500 million dollars" or "$500 million", and stay with one form inside an edition.
- Dates: "November 12", never "11/12". Say "Monday" only together with the date on first use.
- No adjectives the source did not earn: not "massive", "shocking", "landmark". No rhetorical questions. No first person.
- Titles and roles exactly as the source gives them. Do not add credentials from memory.

## Section summaries

- Two sentences that tell the reader what kind of day it was in that section. They are written last, from the items below them, and add no new facts.
- If nothing happened, say so plainly in `quiet_note`.

## Source names

- Story bylines use the outlet alone: "Ars Technica".
- Source lists under a block use "Outlet: topic" in two or three words, so several links from one outlet can be told apart: "Ars Technica: TSMC", "Ukrinform: General Staff update".

## Numbers box

- The value is short ("167", "6.4M", "$1B"). The label is one line, under 100 characters, and says what was counted and when.

## The audio script

- Written for the ear: short sentences, the source said aloud, no lists read as lists.
- It follows the page's order and adds nothing that is not on the page.

## Before filing

Read the edition top to bottom once as the reader would. Cut any sentence that repeats another, any story that appears twice, and any word that only adds emphasis.
